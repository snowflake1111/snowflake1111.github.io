EEG Denoising and Downstream Task Performance

Research Project · Sep 2024 – Jun 2025

PythonEEGSignal ProcessingMachine LearningEEGLAB

EEG denoising is often used to reduce artifacts before machine-learning analysis. In this project, I investigated whether cleaner-looking signals necessarily lead to better downstream task performance.

← Back to all projects

Research Question

How do different EEG denoising approaches influence classification performance across downstream tasks? The project emphasized empirical evaluation rather than assuming that more aggressive denoising is always beneficial.

Methods

  • Worked with BCI ErrP, CHB-MIT seizure, and attention-state datasets.
  • Compared raw EEG with preprocessing and denoising approaches, including filtering, Artifact Subspace Reconstruction (ASR), and IC-U-Net-based processing.
  • Evaluated downstream models including logistic regression, SVM, random forest, LightGBM, MLP, and EEGNet.
  • Performed preprocessing, model training, evaluation, result analysis, and research presentation.

Key Finding

Denoising did not consistently improve downstream classification. In some settings, raw EEG performed as well as or better than denoised signals, highlighting the importance of evaluating preprocessing methods against the target task.
